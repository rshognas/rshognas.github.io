Research

My research sits at the intersection of sociology, social demography, and epidemiology. Using longitudinal cohorts and Nordic register data, I study how conditions at work and in the family shape mental health, mortality, and people's working lives.

Jump to: Themes · Grants & projects · Publications · Presentations · Media

Research themes

Health, ageing, and working life expectancy

How long do people remain in paid work, and who exits early? Using multistate models and Swedish cohort data (including SLOSH), I estimate working life expectancy and how it differs by socioeconomic status, job strain, depressive symptoms, and exposure to workplace bullying or violence. I also study physical activity and wellbeing across the retirement transition.

Job insecurity, job loss, and mental health

I examine how perceived job insecurity, redundancy notices, and job loss affect sleep, depression, anxiety, psychotropic medication use, and sickness absence — and whether support such as Sweden's Employment Security Funds can buffer these consequences.

The psychosocial work environment

Workplace violence, threats, bullying, and illegitimate work tasks are common but understudied stressors. My work, including multicohort studies across the Nordic countries, links these exposures to mental health, sickness absence, and mortality. A new project (JEM-CHILD) extends this to how parents' work stressors relate to children's mental health.

Family demography and the life course

My earlier research, much of it using the Fragile Families study and the 1953 Stockholm Birth Cohort, examines family instability, coparenting, union stability, social support networks, gray divorce and loneliness, and the intergenerational transmission of family behaviour such as early and nonmarital childbearing.
