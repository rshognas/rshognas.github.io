Research

How do social conditions in work and family life contribute to health and well-being over the life course and across generations, and how do these effects differ across social groups?

Jump to: Research areas · Current projects · Selected publications

Research areas

Psychosocial work environments and mental health

How job insecurity, job loss, workplace violence, and other work-related stressors affect mental health, sickness absence, and mortality.

Example: It’s Giving Me the Blues: A Fixed-Effects and G-Formula Approach to Understanding Job Insecurity, Sleep Disturbances, and Major Depression (Social Science & Medicine, 2022)

Working life expectancy

The average number of years people can expect to spend in paid work, and how this differs by education, occupation, income, gender, and working conditions.

Example: Socioeconomic Status and Working Life Expectancy: A Swedish Cohort Study (Journal of Population Ageing, 2026)

Family well-being

How family instability, coparenting, and intergenerational relationships shape the well-being of parents and children, and increasingly how parents’ working conditions reach into family life.

Example: Intergenerational Relationships and Union Stability in Fragile Families (Journal of Marriage and Family, 2010)

Health over the life course

How early-life circumstances and later-life transitions relate to health, well-being, and mortality decades later, using long-running Swedish birth cohorts.

Example: Adolescent Social Isolation and Premature Mortality among Women and Men in a Swedish Birth Cohort (Journal of Population Research, 2020)
