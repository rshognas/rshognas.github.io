Research

My research uses longitudinal surveys, population registers, and linked survey-register data to study how conditions at work and in the family shape health and well-being across the life course.

Jump to: Research areas · Current projects · Selected publications

Research areas

Psychosocial work environments and mental health

I study how job insecurity, job loss, workplace violence and bullying, and illegitimate work tasks affect sleep, depression, anxiety, psychotropic medication use, sickness absence, and mortality — and whether support such as Sweden's Employment Security Funds can buffer these consequences. Recent work includes a multicohort study of workplace violence and mortality across three Nordic countries.

Working life expectancy

How many years can people expect to spend in paid work, and who exits early? Using Swedish population data and multistate models, I estimate working life expectancy and how it differs by education, occupation, income, gender, job strain, depressive symptoms, and exposure to workplace bullying or violence.

Family well-being

My earlier research, much of it using the Fragile Families study, examined family instability, coparenting, union stability, social support, and the intergenerational transmission of family behavior. I am now connecting this work to my research on work and health, asking how parents' working conditions shape children's mental health.

Health over the life course

Using long-running cohorts such as the 1953 Stockholm Birth Cohort, I study how early-life circumstances — including adolescent social isolation and family background — relate to health, mortality, and well-being decades later, as well as transitions in later life such as gray divorce and retirement.
