Docent · Guest Researcher, Epidemiology Unit
Stress Research Institute, Department of Psychology, Stockholm University

I am a sociologist and social demographer studying how work, family, and social relationships shape health across the life course.

My current research focuses on the psychosocial work environment and mental health — including job insecurity, job loss, workplace violence, and illegitimate work tasks — and on how health and socioeconomic conditions shape how long people remain in working life. Earlier work examined family instability, fragile families, and the intergenerational transmission of family behaviour. I work mainly with longitudinal cohort and Swedish register data, using methods such as multistate models, fixed-effects, and the g-formula.

Before moving to Stockholm University in 2017, I was an Assistant Professor of Sociology at the University of Louisville and at California State University, Bakersfield, and a postdoctoral fellow in social demography at the Center for Demography and Ecology, University of Wisconsin–Madison, where I remain a research affiliate.
