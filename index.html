Work Family Health

I received my Ph.D. in Sociology from the University of Illinois at Chicago, and I’m a social epidemiologist whose work lies at the intersection of work, family, and health. My research examines how social conditions contribute to health and well-being over the life course and across generations, with a focus on psychosocial working conditions, working life expectancy, and family relationships. Drawing on longitudinal surveys and Swedish population registers, my work has been published in journals such as Social Science & Medicine, the Journal of Marriage and Family, and the European Journal of Public Health.

I am currently a Guest Researcher at the Stress Research Institute at Stockholm University and Docent in Public Health Sciences. Docent is a Swedish academic qualification recognizing an established, independent research and teaching record. I am also a Research Affiliate at the Center for Demography and Ecology, University of Wisconsin–Madison. Prior to moving to Sweden, I taught courses on quantitative analysis, research methods, and social stratification, among others, at the University of Louisville and California State University, Bakersfield.
