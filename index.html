I am a social epidemiologist with a background in sociology and public health, currently a Guest Researcher at the Stress Research Institute at Stockholm University and Docent in Public Health Sciences. Docent is a Swedish academic qualification indicating an established, independent research and teaching record. Broadly, my work examines how social conditions contribute to health and well-being over the life course, with a focus on work environments, families, social relationships, and socioeconomic inequality.

One line of my current research focuses on psychosocial working conditions and health, including how job insecurity, job loss, workplace violence, and other work-related stressors contribute to mental health and health inequalities. Another line of research examines working life expectancy, using multistate models to study how the average number of years people can expect to spend in paid work differs by education, occupation, income, gender, and psychosocial working conditions.

Teaching and mentoring are important parts of my academic career. I have taught research methods, quantitative analysis, family, and courses on social and health stratification, among others, at the undergraduate and graduate levels. I have also supervised students from the undergraduate through postdoctoral levels. I want to help students learn to ask meaningful questions and evaluate evidence critically, and I try to hold students to high expectations while remaining accessible and responsive to their individual needs and levels of preparation.

Feel free to explore my work, and please don’t hesitate to get in touch!
